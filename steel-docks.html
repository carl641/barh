Standard features

Every steel dock comes with the same three things: a truss drawn for looks as well as strength, a truss or tube frame walkway carrying decorative handrails, and a dock built without a board of lumber in it.

Our steel dock includes a truss that is specifically designed with appearance and strength in mind. This dock takes the standard open gable configuration to the next level!

We offer truss and tube frame walkways. Each option can be arched or flat. Our walkways include sturdy, decorative handrails. This combination of components allows us to build the strongest and visually appealing walkways on the water.

Whether you desire composite or concrete for decking, our steel dock is constructed without the use of lumber. Lumber is commonly used for stringers under decking, fascia board, and bumper mounts by other builders. When you choose Bar H, you get it!

What “fully fabricated” means here

The trusses are cut, fit and welded from steel tube as an assembly — not bolted up from stock angle on site, and not formed from light-gauge sheet. The joints are made once, in the shop, under cover. It is the slower way to build a roof structure and the reason the span stays honest over a wide slip.

Questions we get

Aluminum or steel — how do I choose?

Steel, if first cost matters most and a covered gable-roof slip is what you need. Aluminum, if you want a frame that will never rust, less upkeep over the years, or a style the steel dock does not come in — platform, hip roof, sun deck, commercial or custom.

How wide a slip can the trusses span?

Wider than most private docks need — the span is engineered per build, so tell us the slip width you want and we will draw the truss to it rather than the other way around.

Can a lift go in later?

Yes, and it is cheaper to say so now. If we know a lift is coming, the deck height, the flotation and the clearance under the trusses get drawn for it from the start.

Do you build on Kentucky Lake and Lake Barkley?

Yes — both shores, plus the Cumberland River arm, the Ohio River around Henderson, Owensboro and Evansville, and the Clarksville–Nashville corridor.
