The lake, or the pond out back

Most Hopkinsville docks are one of two jobs: a lake dock on Barkley’s eastern arms, or a dock on a pond or private lake close to home.

Barkley’s eastern arms

The Little River arm and the coves around Cadiz are sheltered from the main lake’s wind — but they’re shallower, and the winter drawdown can leave a cove’s back end on mud.

Corps shoreline

Lake Barkley is Corps water. Private docks need a shoreline permit, and only stretches zoned for them can have one.

Ponds and private lakes

A farm pond or private lake has no current and little level change, and the owner sets the rules. It’s the simplest water there is for a dock.

Hopkinsville dock questions

Do you build docks for Hopkinsville customers?

Yes. Hopkinsville sits on the Clarksville–Nashville corridor we serve, and Lake Barkley is a short drive west.

Will my cove on Lake Barkley go dry in winter?

It can. Barkley drops about five feet for winter, and the back of a shallow cove can go to mud. We site the dock where it still has water at winter pool, or design it to sit safely when it doesn’t.

Is a dock on a farm pond worth building?

If you fish, swim or keep a small boat on it, yes. A platform dock gives you a solid place to do it, and aluminum won’t rot the way pond docks usually do.

Do I need a permit for a pond dock?

On a private pond you own, a federal permit usually isn’t involved. On a shared or association lake, get the owners’ approval in writing first.
