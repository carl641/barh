Part river, part lake

Lake Barkley’s pool reaches all the way up the Cumberland past Clarksville, so a dock here is managed like a lake dock but lives with a river’s current.

Lake levels, river current

The water follows Lake Barkley’s seasonal levels — higher in summer, lower in winter — but at Clarksville it still flows, and it carries drift when it’s up.

The Red River joins here

The Red meets the Cumberland in Clarksville and brings its own floodwater. When both are high at once, as in 2010, the water rises fast.

The lake is close

Dover and the open water of Lake Barkley are about forty minutes west, and many Clarksville families keep their boat there.

Clarksville dock questions

Do you build boat docks in Clarksville?

Yes. Clarksville is part of the Clarksville–Nashville corridor, home to a substantial share of our customers.

Is the Cumberland at Clarksville treated as a river or a lake?

Both, in a way. It’s the upper end of Lake Barkley’s pool, managed by the Corps’ Nashville District, so lake-style shoreline rules apply — but the dock still has to be engineered for current and flood.

We’re at Fort Campbell and may move in a few years. Is a dock worth it?

Often, yes. A low-upkeep dock gets used for as long as you’re there and adds appeal when the house sells. Permits stay with the owner, not the house, so the buyer applies for their own — keep your paperwork tidy for them.

Can you build at Lake Barkley near Dover?

Yes — see Kentucky Lake & Lake Barkley.
