What sets the Timber Series apart

Three things decide how a covered dock lives on this water: the roof, the frame under it, and whether either was drawn for your shoreline.

Trusses, not bent sheet

The roof structure is fully fabricated from steel tube. That is what lets the Timber hold a clean span across a wide slip without a forest of posts in the water where your boat has to go.

The gable earns its keep

Two slopes give you the most headroom over the slip for the least structure overhead — room for a tower, a T-top or a lift to travel, and a straightforward roof to re-skin twenty years from now.

Bold and timeless on the water

The gable end is the face of the dock. It is the profile people picture when they picture a covered slip, and it stays right long after a trend-driven shape stops looking new.

What “fully fabricated” means here

The trusses are cut, fit and welded from steel tube as an assembly — not bolted up from stock angle on site, and not formed from light-gauge sheet. The joints are made once, in the shop, under cover. It is the slower way to build a roof structure and the reason the span stays honest over a wide slip.

Questions we get

Gable or hip — how do I choose?

Gable, if you want the most headroom over the slip and the classic covered-dock profile. Hip, if the dock sits on open water where wind hits the end wall, or you simply want the look. The Premier Series is our hip-roof dock; the frame work behind both is the same idea.

How wide a slip can the trusses span?

Wider than most private docks need — the span is engineered per build, so tell us the slip width you want and we will draw the truss to it rather than the other way around.

Can a lift go in later?

Yes, and it is cheaper to say so now. If we know a lift is coming, the deck height, the flotation and the clearance under the trusses get drawn for it from the start.

Do you build on Kentucky Lake and Lake Barkley?

Yes — both shores, plus the Cumberland River arm, the Ohio River around Henderson, Owensboro and Evansville, and the Clarksville–Nashville corridor.
