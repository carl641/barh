Built for a river, not a lake

The Ohio doesn't hold a summer level the way a lake does. It rises and falls with the seasons, and it moves, so a dock here is engineered for the water it will actually see.

It rises

At Evansville the river has climbed more than 30 feet between low water and a spring crest. Anchoring is specified for the depth and the yearly swing at your lot, not the level on the day we measure.

It runs

Unlike a lake, the Ohio has a current, and high water carries drift down it. Where the dock sits on your bank matters as much as how it is built.

It needs a permit

The Ohio is a navigable river, so a dock on it needs approval from the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, and it can need a state permit as well.

Questions we get

Do you build docks on the Ohio River?

Yes. The Ohio around Henderson, Owensboro and Evansville is home base — we're based in Henderson County, and most of our customers live along this stretch of the river.

Does a dock on the Ohio need a permit?

Yes. The Ohio is a navigable river, so a new dock needs approval from the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, and it can need a state permit too. Tell us where the lot is when you ask for a quote.
