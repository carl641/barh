What the Ohio does in front of an Evansville lot

Three things shape every river dock we draw for Vanderburgh County: who owns the water, how far it moves, and what moves on it.

The river is Kentucky’s

Kentucky’s border runs along the Ohio’s old low-water line on the Indiana bank, so most of the river in front of an Evansville lot is legally Kentucky. A dock reaching out from the Indiana shore can bring agencies from both states into the conversation, alongside the Corps.

A thirty-foot swing

The Evansville gauge has ranged from about 13 feet at low pool to a 47.67-foot crest in April 2025. Anchoring, gangway length and how the dock rides up with the water are sized for that range, not for a summer afternoon.

Towboats and drift

This is a working stretch of river. Tow wakes reach the bank day and night, and spring high water carries whole trees. Where on your bank the dock sits — and what shields it — matters as much as how it’s built.

Planning a dock in Evansville

Before anything is drawn, these are the questions we work through for an Evansville river lot. Having the answers ready makes the first conversation a lot shorter.

Start with a quote
  • Corps approvalAny structure in the Ohio needs sign-off from the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, Louisville District, under Section 10 of the Rivers and Harbors Act.
  • The floodwayMost river lots in Vanderburgh County sit in the Ohio’s floodway, where Indiana DNR can require a construction-in-a-floodway permit for work on the bank.
  • Bank heightThe taller and steeper the bank, the longer the gangway has to be to stay walkable at both low pool and high water. Photos taken at low water help most.
  • Your boatsPontoon, bass boat or cruiser — slip width, depth and lift choice all start from what you’ll keep in it.

Evansville dock questions

Do you build boat docks on the Ohio River in Evansville?

Yes. Evansville and Newburgh are part of the Ohio River Valley we serve from Providence, Kentucky — this stretch of river is where Tristan grew up.

Do I need an Indiana permit, a Kentucky permit, or both?

It depends on the lot and how far the dock reaches. Corps approval is needed for any dock in the Ohio; Indiana DNR can require a floodway permit for the work on the bank; and because the river itself is Kentucky to roughly the Indiana low-water line, Kentucky can be involved too. Tell us the address and we’ll talk through which apply before anything is drawn.

What happens to a dock when the Ohio floods?

A river dock is engineered either to rise with the water on its anchors or to be secured for it — which suits your lot depends on the bank and the current there. Aluminum comes out of floodwater the way it went in: no swelling, no rot, nothing to replace once the debris is cleared.

We’re on a private lake near Evansville, not the river. Is that different?

Much simpler. A lake has no current and holds a steady level, a federal permit usually isn’t involved, and the lake’s owner or association sets the rules. Get their approval in writing before we draw the dock.
