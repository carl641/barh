Two rivers meet above town

The Green River joins the Ohio just upstream of Henderson. Which one your lot is on changes how the dock is built.

The Ohio

The main river is wide, carries barge traffic, and rises tens of feet in a wet spring. Docks here are anchored for the high water and set where tow wakes and drift do the least harm.

The Green

Narrower and slower, the Green is gentler day to day — but when the Ohio is high it backs up into the Green, so a Green River dock still has to handle the Ohio’s floods.

Close to the shop

Planning a dock in Henderson

A Henderson dock usually comes down to the same handful of decisions. Here they are, in the order we work through them.

Start with a quote
  • Which riverThe Ohio and the navigable lower Green both fall under the Corps’ Louisville District, which approves structures in them under Section 10.
  • Kentucky floodplainMost river lots sit in the floodplain, where the Kentucky Division of Water can require its own permit for the work.
  • Bluff or bottomParts of Henderson sit high above the water and parts are low bottomland. The height of your bank decides the gangway and the stairs, and whether the dock is reachable at high water.
  • How far outFar enough to keep the slip in water at low pool, without pushing into the channel or the path of passing tows.

Henderson dock questions

Do you build boat docks in Henderson?

Yes. Henderson is where Tristan grew up, and Henderson County is home ground for us — our shop is about 30 miles away in Providence.

Can you build on the Green River as well as the Ohio?

Yes. The Green is calmer day to day, but it backs up when the Ohio is high, so we engineer a Green River dock for the Ohio’s flood levels too.

How far out into the river does a dock need to reach?

Far enough that the slip stays in water at low pool, and no farther than it needs to — the Corps reviews how far a structure extends, and the channel side of the river is where tows pass.

We’re between Henderson and Providence. Is that in range?

Very much so. Henderson, Webster and Hopkins counties are the closest work we do.
