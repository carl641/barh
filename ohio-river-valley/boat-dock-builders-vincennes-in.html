Boat dock builders in Vincennes, Indiana

Vincennes sits on the Wabash, not the Ohio — and the lower Wabash has no dam to hold it at a pool. A dock here is built for a river that ranges freely from late-summer lows to spring floods, or it goes on one of the area’s many private lakes.

A river with no pool

Most rivers we build on are held at a pool by locks and dams. The Wabash past Vincennes isn’t, and that changes the dock.

No pool to hold it

With no dam below Vincennes, the Wabash simply follows the weather: low and slow in a dry autumn, and well over fifteen feet higher in a wet spring. The dock has to work at both ends.

Sand that moves

The Wabash runs over sand, and its bars and banks shift from year to year. Anchoring is chosen for a bottom that scours, and the dock is placed where the channel has stayed put.

Lakes on old mine ground

Southwest Indiana’s coal country left deep, steep-sided pit lakes behind. The water is still, but it can drop to real depth a few feet from the bank — which changes how a dock is anchored.

Planning a dock around Vincennes

River or lake, a dock in Knox County starts with knowing the water at its lowest and at its highest. These are the questions we’ll ask.

Start with a quote
  • Indiana DNRIndiana treats the Wabash as a navigable waterway, so building on its bed and banks goes through Indiana DNR, and the Corps may need to sign off too.
  • Low-water accessTell us how far the water pulls back from your bank in late summer. That sets how far out the dock reaches and whether it floats or stands.
  • Who owns the lakeOn a private, association or pit lake, the owner sets the rules. Get their approval in writing before the dock is designed.
  • Depth at the bankOn a pit lake, a few depth readings off the bank tell us more than anything else about how the dock is anchored.

Vincennes dock questions

Do you build docks in Vincennes?

Yes. Vincennes and Knox County are the northern edge of the Ohio River Valley area we serve from Providence, Kentucky.

Can a dock work on the Wabash with no steady pool?

Yes, but it’s designed differently from a dock on a held pool. It has to reach usable water in a dry autumn and survive the spring rise, so the anchoring, walkway and the dock’s position on the bank all come from your lot’s low and high water.

What’s different about a dock on a strip-pit lake?

The water is calm, but the bottom often drops away steeply close to shore. That can make a floating dock the better choice, and it changes how it’s anchored. A few depth readings off your bank answer most of it.
